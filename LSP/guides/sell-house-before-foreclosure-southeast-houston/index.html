Selling a House Before Foreclosure in Southeast Houston

A practical starting point for southeast Houston owners considering a sale with pending foreclosure: deadlines, property details, payoffs, and local resources.

By Lone Star Property Solutions · Updated November 9, 2026

The useful starting point

A possible sale needs to be evaluated against the actual notice, available time, title, payoffs, and required approvals. A submission, conversation, or offer from Lone Star does not stop foreclosure or change a sale date. Contact your servicer and an attorney promptly about the notice and your options.

In this guide1. Establish what is happening now2. Find the correct county resource3. Evaluate a possible purchase realistically4. Keep your other conversations moving

1. Establish what is happening now

Being behind on payments, receiving a notice, and having a scheduled foreclosure sale are different situations. Keep the actual correspondence together and ask your servicer or attorney to explain its significance. Do not build a plan around an assumed grace period.

  • Record the date and sender on each notice.
  • Distinguish a preferred move date from a scheduled sale date.
  • Confirm the current status through independently verified contacts.

2. Find the correct county resource

Use the property’s county, not just its mailing city. Harris County provides a trustee-notice search; Galveston County provides separate foreclosure resources; Brazoria County explains its notice-record process. A notice search does not replace advice about your case.

  • Start with the exact street address and parcel records.
  • Identify whether the issue involves the mortgage, property taxes, or another lien.
  • Ask the responsible office about record-search questions.

3. Evaluate a possible purchase realistically

Tell Rickey the address, condition, occupancy, and known deadline. A proposed purchase still needs property review, title work, verified amounts, and agreed written terms. An attractive offer is not evidence that a transaction can finish in time.

  • Identify other owners and existing representation.
  • Have the closing professional confirm required payoff information.
  • Review costs and conditions alongside the proposed price.

4. Keep your other conversations moving

A buyer evaluates a purchase. Your servicer, housing counselor, and attorney address different questions. Continue those conversations while considering whether selling fits your goals; do not wait for a buyer’s response before dealing with a notice.

  • Ask the servicer about available options for your account.
  • Use the CFPB mortgage-help resources to locate appropriate help.
  • Do not send account numbers, court documents, or IDs through the property form.

Questions Homeowners Ask

Can I sell my house if foreclosure is pending?+

A sale may be possible, but it depends on your circumstances, authority to sell, title, payoffs, approvals, and time available. Have your attorney and closing professional evaluate the requirements before relying on a proposed closing.

Does contacting Rickey stop the foreclosure?+

No. Contacting a buyer, submitting a property, or receiving an offer does not stop foreclosure or extend a deadline.

Is pre-foreclosure the same as a completed foreclosure?+

No. Describe what has actually occurred and share the known status with your advisers. Do not assume you retain authority to sell after a foreclosure sale.

Where do I look for a Harris County trustee notice?+

Use the Harris County Clerk’s trustee-notice search linked below. Match the property carefully; the search is not a complete review of your legal situation.

Can I submit a house that needs repairs?+

Yes. Describe known repairs, occupancy, and safe access. A submission begins review and does not guarantee an offer or a closing date.

What if the sale date is very close?+

Contact your attorney and servicer promptly. Tell Rickey the known date, but do not rely on an unconfirmed purchase as your deadline plan.
