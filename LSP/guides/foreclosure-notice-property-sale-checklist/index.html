Received a Foreclosure Notice? A Property Sale Preparation Checklist

What southeast Houston homeowners can gather after a foreclosure notice before discussing a possible property sale with a buyer and their advisers.

By Lone Star Property Solutions · Updated November 9, 2026

The useful starting point

Keep the notice, confirm its meaning and dates with your attorney or servicer, and assemble a basic property summary. For an initial buyer inquiry, share the address, your role, known condition, occupancy, and deadline—not private loan or identity documents.

In this guide1. Make a simple notice log2. Prepare a one-page property summary3. Keep the financial review with the right people4. Decide what you need from each conversation

1. Make a simple notice log

Write down what you received, when you received it, and who sent it. Keep originals for the professionals advising you. An organized log helps you explain the situation without repeatedly forwarding sensitive paperwork.

  • Separate letters from lenders, attorneys, and potential buyers.
  • Mark dates as confirmed or unconfirmed.
  • Use contact details independently verified with the relevant organization.

2. Prepare a one-page property summary

An owner in Pasadena and an owner in Dickinson may have different county records and local departments to contact. Start with the exact property rather than assuming the same process applies everywhere in southeast Houston.

  • Street address, city, county if known, and property type.
  • Owner, agent, or other authorized-contact role.
  • Current condition, occupancy, access constraints, and known date.

3. Keep the financial review with the right people

Amounts on an old statement may not be the amounts required for a transaction. Ask your servicer and closing professional what current information they need. Do not guess at net proceeds from the purchase price alone.

  • List known mortgages, taxes, and other title concerns for professional review.
  • Keep estimates visibly separate from confirmed amounts.
  • Use a secure channel approved by the recipient for private documents.

4. Decide what you need from each conversation

A purchase review should help establish whether a buyer has a feasible proposal. It should not replace the advice you need on a notice. Write down unanswered questions and the person responsible for each answer.

  • Attorney or servicer: meaning and status of the notice.
  • Closing professional: title, payoff, and closing requirements.
  • Buyer: property fit, proposed terms, and remaining review.

Questions Homeowners Ask

Should I upload my foreclosure notice to the initial form?+

No. State the known date and situation in your own words. Keep notices containing personal or account information for an appropriate secure exchange with your advisers.

Do I need every document before contacting a buyer?+

No. The address, your role, condition, occupancy, and known deadline can start a review. Missing information still needs to be resolved before a transaction.

Should I clean out the house first?+

You can discuss the current condition before arranging a cleanout. Do not spend money or enter unsafe areas merely to prepare an inquiry.

Can a relative submit the property?+

An authorized contact can introduce it and explain their relationship. Submitting information does not establish authority to sign a sale agreement.

Which county office should I use?+

Use the property’s actual county. Mailing-city names and postal addresses alone can be misleading; check available parcel records.

What if different letters show different dates?+

Do not choose a date by guesswork. Ask your attorney or servicer to resolve the discrepancy promptly and explain what action is needed.
