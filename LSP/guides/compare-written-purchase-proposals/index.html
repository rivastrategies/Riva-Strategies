Compare written house purchase proposals

A practical way to compare written house purchase proposals in southeast Houston: seller costs, payoffs, closing dates, possession, and unresolved conditions.

By Lone Star Property Solutions · Updated November 9, 2026

The useful starting point

Compare what is written, what you would pay, and what must happen before the transaction can close. Keep unknown amounts visible. A higher price alone does not explain the costs or conditions attached to a proposal.

In this guide1. Put the numbers on the same basis2. Read the conditions alongside the price3. Separate the important dates4. Resolve questions before choosing

1. Put the numbers on the same basis

Use the price in each written proposal. Separately record additional credits to you, costs assigned to you, credits you give the buyer, your repair or cleanout spending, other identified costs, and payoffs. The comparison worksheet performs only the arithmetic you supply.

  • Enter zero only when you have determined that a line is zero or does not apply.
  • Count each cost once. A concession already deducted from the price should not be deducted a second time.
  • Record the source and date of an estimate, and flag amounts that still need confirmation.

2. Read the conditions alongside the price

Texas Real Estate Commission’s resale form addresses financing, condition, title, closing, possession, and expenses separately. Your actual agreement and any addenda determine the terms of your transaction; do not assume two proposals use the same conditions.

  • What financing or other conditions remain unresolved?
  • What inspection, termination, repair, or access terms are proposed?
  • Which approvals or documents are still needed, and who is responsible for them?

3. Separate the important dates

Write down the proposed closing date and possession date separately. Note any other dates identified in the proposal and who supplied them. If a date is important because of a move, lease, or another obligation, make that constraint part of the discussion.

  • Distinguish a preferred date from a date in an agreed contract.
  • Ask how a change in timing would affect responsibilities or costs.
  • An inquiry, worksheet, or unsigned proposal does not establish a closing commitment.

4. Resolve questions before choosing

TDI encourages reviewing closing charges and requesting the closing papers in advance. Ask for clarification when an expense is unclear. Use the appropriate real estate, title, legal, or tax professional for questions that require interpretation of your documents.

  • Keep a list of unanswered questions for each proposal.
  • Review the written responsibilities for repairs, contents, and possession.
  • Reconcile the final settlement figures with the terms and updated amounts; this worksheet does not replace them.

Questions Homeowners Ask

Is the highest offer always the best choice?+

The price alone does not explain costs, conditions, timing, or possession. Compare the full written proposals against your priorities.

What if the payoff is unknown?+

Keep it marked as unknown. Obtain transaction-specific information through your closing professional and servicer before relying on net proceeds.

Does the worksheet determine my house’s value?+

No. It compares only the figures you enter and is not a valuation or a verified settlement statement.

Can an offer guarantee a foreclosure deadline will be met?+

Do not rely on a proposed date without confirming feasibility with your advisers. An offer does not stop foreclosure.

Are my worksheet numbers sent to Rickey?+

The comparison worksheet operates in your browser and does not submit the financial figures. A separate property inquiry is required to start a purchase review.

What should I clarify before choosing a proposal?+

Ask about seller costs, payoffs, financing and other conditions, inspections, closing, possession, and any unresolved approvals. Have appropriate advisers review terms you do not understand.
