Selling a House During Bankruptcy in Texas

Questions southeast Houston homeowners can take to a bankruptcy attorney before discussing a house sale, including authority, approvals, proceeds, and timing.

By Lone Star Property Solutions · Updated November 9, 2026

The useful starting point

If you are in bankruptcy or considering filing, speak with your bankruptcy attorney before signing a sale agreement or transferring property. The case, property status, and required approvals affect what can happen. Lone Star evaluates possible purchases; it does not provide bankruptcy representation.

In this guide1. Explain the proposed sale to your attorney2. Separate purchase interest from permission to proceed3. Assemble the property facts4. Coordinate the next step through your advisers

1. Explain the proposed sale to your attorney

Federal bankruptcy involves different chapters and case-specific rules. The U.S. Courts’ Bankruptcy Basics explains the chapters in general terms. A buyer cannot determine your authority to sell, exemptions, or treatment of proceeds from an address and a price.

  • Ask who has authority over a proposed sale.
  • Ask what trustee involvement, court authorization, or other process is required.
  • Ask how a sale could affect your case and proceeds.

2. Separate purchase interest from permission to proceed

A potential buyer’s willingness to review a house is not approval to sell it. Tell Rickey that bankruptcy affects the property, without posting case files or financial schedules in the inquiry.

  • Use your attorney’s instructions for any proposed agreement.
  • Identify approvals that remain outstanding.
  • Avoid promising a closing date before requirements are understood.

3. Assemble the property facts

Condition, occupancy, access, and location still matter to a possible purchase. Southeast Houston includes multiple counties; use the full address to identify the correct property and records. County property information does not establish bankruptcy-sale authority.

  • Describe known repairs and any lease.
  • Identify all owners and existing representation.
  • Provide photographs only when safe and authorized.

4. Coordinate the next step through your advisers

Keep the proposed price, conditions, and timing together for review. Do not transfer a deed or rely on informal promises about keeping proceeds or resolving debt. Your attorney should address what is permitted in your case.

  • Ask how a proposal should be presented.
  • Confirm the process for sharing documents securely.
  • Keep legal advice separate from the buyer’s evaluation.

Questions Homeowners Ask

Can I sell a house during Chapter 7 or Chapter 13 bankruptcy?+

The answer depends on the case and property. Ask your bankruptcy attorney who has authority and what approvals or procedures are required before agreeing to a sale.

Should I tell the buyer about the bankruptcy?+

Tell the buyer that bankruptcy may affect a transaction and coordinate through your attorney. Do not upload private bankruptcy schedules or financial records to the initial form.

Does an offer mean the court will approve a sale?+

No. A buyer’s offer does not establish permission, satisfy court requirements, or guarantee approval.

Will I keep the sale proceeds?+

Do not assume so. Ask your attorney about the treatment of proceeds, liens, exemptions, and obligations in your particular case.

Can Rickey recommend whether I should file bankruptcy?+

Lone Star’s role is evaluating a property purchase. Decisions about filing or managing bankruptcy belong with qualified legal counsel.

Can I agree to a fast closing?+

A date is only workable if the necessary reviews and approvals allow it. Have your attorney review timing and conditions before committing.
