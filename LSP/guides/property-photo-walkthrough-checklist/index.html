A property photo and walkthrough checklist

Prepare a useful property photo package for a southeast Houston home: exterior views, condition details, repair history, occupancy, and authorized walkthrough access.

By Lone Star Property Solutions · Updated November 9, 2026

The useful starting point

Start with a clear front exterior, a representative interior, and the main concern you want to explain. Add the photo date and what you actually observed. Photos help introduce a property; they do not establish its full condition or replace a professional inspection.

In this guide1. Show enough context2. Explain a concern with two views3. Plan a useful walkthrough4. Choose the first four files

1. Show enough context

Photograph from safe, already accessible places. You do not need to climb onto a roof, enter a crawlspace, open an electrical panel, or operate equipment to prepare an initial submission.

  • Front exterior, including enough context to recognize the property
  • Rear exterior or yard, only if safely accessible and you are authorized to enter
  • Main living space, kitchen, bathrooms, and any unfinished rooms
  • Property address, photo date, and your relationship to the property in your accompanying notes

2. Explain a concern with two views

Use a wider photo to show where the concern is, then a closer photo to show what you observed. Describe visible conditions without guessing at the cause or the cost of repair.

  • For example: “Rear bedroom ceiling stain; first noticed after rain; repair history unknown.”
  • Note when the issue was observed and whether a professional has already evaluated it.
  • Identify existing estimates, invoices, or repair records without treating an estimate as completed work.

3. Plan a useful walkthrough

State whether the property is vacant, owner-occupied, or tenant-occupied. Identify the authorized contact for arranging access and any spaces that will not be available. An initial photo package is separate from a licensed inspection, which has its own scope and limitations.

  • Coordinate appointment windows through the appropriate owner, agent, or authorized contact.
  • Describe access limitations or known concerns before a visit.
  • Keep keys, alarm details, lockbox codes, and tenant identity information out of the public inquiry form.

4. Choose the first four files

The submission form accepts up to four JPEG, PNG, WebP, or PDF files, each up to 5 MB. Choose a small package that explains the property. If you already have more useful material, you can include a permission-controlled HTTPS folder link you are authorized to share.

  • A clear front exterior
  • A representative interior space
  • The principal condition concern or unfinished area
  • One useful existing document, or another relevant photo
  • Before sharing, remove visible people, IDs, mail, account information, and other private details.
