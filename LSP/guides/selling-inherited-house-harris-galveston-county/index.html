Selling an Inherited House in Harris or Galveston County

A practical inherited-home checklist for southeast Houston families: authority, occupancy, contents, repairs, records, and preparing for a possible sale.

By Lone Star Property Solutions · Updated November 9, 2026

The useful starting point

Start by identifying the property and asking an estate attorney or appropriate title professional who can authorize a sale. Then gather condition, occupancy, and access details. A family relationship or a submitted inquiry does not by itself establish authority to sell.

In this guide1. Confirm who can make the decision2. Gather facts before paying for a cleanout3. Use the correct local records4. Compare the options as a group

1. Confirm who can make the decision

An inherited house can involve an estate, multiple people, or unresolved ownership questions. The Texas State Law Library’s probate guide is a starting resource, not an answer for a particular estate. Get case-specific advice before signing.

  • Identify the people and representatives already involved.
  • Ask what documents establish authority.
  • Do not assume one relative can sign for everyone.

2. Gather facts before paying for a cleanout

Describe belongings, repairs, occupancy, and utility status as they stand. A buyer can begin evaluating the current condition without a polished presentation. Protect personal papers and sentimental items when planning any access.

  • Record safe areas that can be photographed.
  • Identify items excluded from a potential sale.
  • Discuss cleanout responsibility in any proposed terms.

3. Use the correct local records

A Friendswood mailing address can involve Harris or Galveston County; nearby communities can cross other county lines. Use the specific address and estate information to locate the correct records. A property-search result is not proof of sale authority.

  • Locate available parcel and deed references.
  • Keep estate records with the professionals handling them.
  • Separate property records from the estate’s legal requirements.

4. Compare the options as a group

Bring the proposed price, costs, timing, and responsibilities into one conversation. If different people need information, circulate the same written proposal through the authorized representative rather than relying on separate verbal accounts.

  • Mark unresolved decisions clearly.
  • Discuss possession and access before scheduling work.
  • Have appropriate advisers review the proposed transaction.

Questions Homeowners Ask

Can I contact Rickey before probate is finished?+

You can describe the property and explain the situation. Ask your attorney what discussions or agreements are appropriate and what authority or approvals a sale would require.

Do all heirs have to sign?+

That depends on ownership and the estate circumstances. Have the appropriate legal and title professionals establish who must participate.

Do we need to empty the house first?+

You can discuss a purchase before a cleanout. Any responsibility for remaining belongings needs to be agreed in writing.

What if someone still lives there?+

Describe occupancy and any known agreement. Do not promise vacant possession or arrange access without the necessary authority.

Does the tax record settle ownership?+

No. A tax or appraisal record is not a substitute for estate and title review.

Can we submit photos instead of estate documents?+

Safe, authorized property photos can help an initial review. Keep private estate, identity, and financial documents out of the initial inquiry.
