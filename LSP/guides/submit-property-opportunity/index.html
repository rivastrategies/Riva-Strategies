What to include in a property opportunity submission

A practical checklist for homeowners, agents, and deal partners submitting a southeast Houston residential property opportunity.

By Lone Star Property Solutions · Updated November 9, 2026

The useful starting point

Start with the full property address, your relationship to the property, its current condition and occupancy, and a way to contact you. Add asking terms, photos, and available records when you have them.

In this guide1. Identify the property and your role2. Describe condition without guessing3. Explain occupancy, access, and timing4. Add a small, useful evidence package

1. Identify the property and your role

Use the street address, city, and ZIP. If you have a parcel or appraisal-account number, include it in your notes. Explain whether you own the home, represent the seller, hold a contractual interest, or are another authorized contact.

  • List other decision-makers when relevant, without sharing their private contact details unnecessarily.
  • Explain any existing listing or contractual arrangement.
  • Do not submit documents or photos you are not authorized to share.

2. Describe condition without guessing

Separate what you have observed from what a contractor or inspector has documented. “Roof leak in the rear bedroom” is more useful than an unsupported total renovation estimate.

  • Known roof, foundation, plumbing, electrical, or HVAC concerns
  • Unfinished rooms, additions, or conversions
  • Known water damage and repairs already completed

3. Explain occupancy, access, and timing

Let the buyer know whether the property is occupied or vacant, whether a lease is in place, and how an authorized walkthrough could be coordinated. Share important dates early; an inquiry itself does not change any deadline.

  • Do not include gate or lockbox codes in the initial form.
  • If tenants are present, preserve their privacy and follow applicable access arrangements.
  • Identify preferred timing separately from contractual or legal deadlines.

4. Add a small, useful evidence package

A few clear exterior and interior photos, an asking price if known, and available records can make the first conversation more productive. Label estimates and projections as estimates rather than verified outcomes.

  • Photograph only safe, accessible areas.
  • Avoid IDs, account statements, personal correspondence, and tenant information in photos.
  • Link a permission-controlled document folder only when you are authorized to share its contents.

Put the details to use.

Start a submission with what you know. Optional fields and records can add context.
