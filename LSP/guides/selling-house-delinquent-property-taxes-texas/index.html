Selling a House With Delinquent Property Taxes in Southeast Houston

Prepare for a possible sale with delinquent property taxes, tax liens, or tax notices in Harris, Galveston, or Brazoria County.

By Lone Star Property Solutions · Updated November 9, 2026

The useful starting point

Identify the correct taxing and collecting offices, confirm the account’s status, and have a closing professional review the amounts and title requirements. A tax bill, appraisal record, and title report answer different questions. A buyer inquiry does not stop collection or a tax sale.

In this guide1. Identify the property and collecting offices2. Confirm status before relying on a balance3. Explore your options with the responsible office4. Evaluate the proposed transaction

1. Identify the property and collecting offices

Begin with the address, county, and any parcel reference you already have. Pearland and Friendswood illustrate why a mailing-city name alone is not enough. An appraisal district is not automatically the office handling every payment or collection question.

  • Use official records to confirm the correct account.
  • Check who issued the notice or bill.
  • Ask the collecting office about account-specific questions.

2. Confirm status before relying on a balance

A previously printed balance may not represent the amount needed for a closing. Ask the responsible office and closing professional what current information is required. If you have received legal correspondence, involve your attorney promptly.

  • Keep notices and correspondence available for secure professional review.
  • Distinguish a tax concern from a mortgage foreclosure.
  • Do not rely on the form submission to change a deadline.

3. Explore your options with the responsible office

Harris County publishes property-tax and payment-arrangement resources. Availability and requirements depend on the account. Galveston County separately explains tax-sale and mortgage-foreclosure information. Ask the appropriate office about your situation rather than assuming one county’s process applies elsewhere.

  • Ask about any account-specific requirements.
  • Confirm the current status of any pending action.
  • Keep buyer discussions separate from collection questions.

4. Evaluate the proposed transaction

Compare the purchase price with verified payoffs, seller costs, and remaining conditions. Lone Star may review a property with a tax concern, but it does not promise to remove liens or settle a tax dispute.

  • Disclose known title concerns early.
  • Have the appropriate professionals verify required amounts.
  • Review the written agreement and closing figures before committing.

Questions Homeowners Ask

Can I introduce a house with unpaid property taxes?+

Yes. Disclose what you know. Whether a purchase can proceed depends on title, required amounts, approvals, timing, and agreed terms.

Is the appraisal value what a buyer will pay?+

No. An appraisal record is a property reference. It is not a purchase offer or a transaction-specific valuation.

Will the buyer automatically pay all my taxes?+

Do not assume that. The agreement, title requirements, and closing arrangements determine how amounts are handled.

Can I arrange payments instead of selling?+

Ask the responsible collecting office what options apply to your account. A property buyer cannot determine your eligibility.

Does a tax sale follow the same process as a mortgage foreclosure?+

Do not treat them as interchangeable. Identify the type of notice and speak with the responsible office and your attorney.

What should I share in the initial inquiry?+

The property address, your role, condition, occupancy, and a brief description of the known tax issue or deadline. Keep sensitive records out of the initial upload.
