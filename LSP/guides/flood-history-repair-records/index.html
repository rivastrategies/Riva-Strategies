Flood history, repair records, and elevation certificates

A southeast Houston property submission checklist for known water damage, repair records, mapped flood information, and available elevation certificates.

By Lone Star Property Solutions · Updated November 9, 2026

The useful starting point

Share known water damage and completed repairs separately from a property’s mapped flood information. Include available permits, remediation records, and elevation certificates, and identify what remains unknown.

In this guideSeparate mapped information from property historyDescribe the damage and the responseAsk about records before starting major work

Separate mapped information from property history

FEMA’s Flood Map Service Center provides official flood-map information. Local departments also publish guidance. A map result is not a complete assessment of a property’s condition, history, or future risk.

  • Use the exact address when researching a map.
  • Identify whether information is current, preliminary, or draft.
  • Do not describe a home as flood-free based only on a map.

Describe the damage and the response

If there was water intrusion, explain what you know: when it occurred, which areas were affected, and what work followed. It is better to distinguish known facts from missing records than to fill gaps with assumptions.

  • Available cleanup or remediation documentation
  • Repair invoices and permit records
  • Areas not yet evaluated or repaired

Ask about records before starting major work

League City provides a process for requesting final elevation certificates. Dickinson’s development guidance describes how substantial repairs or improvements can affect applicable requirements. Confirm what applies with the responsible department before relying on a renovation plan.

  • Share a certificate if one is already available.
  • Keep repair estimates separate from approvals or permits.
  • Use qualified professionals for structural, environmental, insurance, and legal questions.

Questions Homeowners Ask

Can I submit a flood-damaged house in Dickinson or League City?+

Yes. Describe known damage and completed work. Each property is evaluated individually; submission does not guarantee a purchase.

Does a flood map show whether a house has ever flooded?+

No. Mapped information and the property’s history answer different questions. Share known events and available repair records separately.

Should I repair everything before contacting Rickey?+

You can discuss the current condition first. Get appropriate professional advice before beginning major work.

What records help with a Clear Lake-area property?+

Available damage photographs, remediation and repair records, permits, surveys, and elevation certificates can help. Identify the property’s actual jurisdiction.

Does an elevation certificate replace an inspection?+

No. It serves a different purpose. Use qualified professionals for condition and property-specific assessments.

What if I do not know the full damage history?+

Say what is known and what is unknown. Do not describe the house as flood-free or fully repaired without support.
