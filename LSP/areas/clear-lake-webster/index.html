What to know locally

The exact address comes first.

Houston identifies Clear Lake as one of its super neighborhoods. Webster is a separate municipality with its own local guidance. Webster’s flood-protection information discusses drainage, Clear Creek, and storm surge. Use the property’s actual jurisdiction and records rather than treating the entire Clear Lake area as one permitting district.

Before you take on the next project

A flood map and a property’s history answer different questions. A map is a starting point for understanding mapped hazards; it does not replace the owner’s knowledge, inspections, or repair records. Sharing what is known and unknown helps the review begin with fewer assumptions.

Build a clearer submission

  • Provide the full address and the municipality, if known.
  • Share known flooding, water intrusion, and remediation history.
  • Include an elevation certificate or survey if one is available.
  • Note HOA information, occupancy, and access requirements.

Available records are helpful. Unknown details can be discussed during review.

Property questions

Start with clarity.

Can I submit a property that has had water damage?+

Yes. Describe when the damage occurred, which areas were affected, and repairs you know were completed. Include available records and photos. Each property is reviewed individually; submitting does not guarantee an offer.

Can an agent or deal partner submit a property here?+

Yes. Include your role, representation or contractual interest, asking terms, and information you are authorized to share. Any fees, assignments, and transaction conditions require separate review and agreement.
